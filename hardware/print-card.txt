BUCK PRINT CARD
========================================
printer : Flashforge Creator 5 Pro 0.4 nozzle
process : 0.24mm Standard @FF C5
layer   : 0.24 mm
mode    : one filament per plate

PLATES
  1. brown (PETG #8A5A36, about 80 g at 40 percent infill): head, jaw, ear R, ear L
  2. bone (PETG #EAE0CA, about 6 g at 40 percent infill): antler R, antler L
  3. black (PETG #1A1A1A, about 5 g at 40 percent infill): nose, eye R, eye L
  4. walnut (PETG #3B2618, about 148 g at 40 percent infill): mount, hook L, hook R, pivot pin

ORIENTATION (no supports anywhere)
  head      : neck rim on the bed
  jaw       : mouth face on the bed
  mount     : flat back on the bed
  nose      : cut face on the bed
  eye R     : flat back on the bed, dome up
  eye L     : flat back on the bed, dome up
  antler R  : flat side on the bed
  antler L  : flat side on the bed
  ear R     : lying on its flat back, cup up
  ear L     : lying on its flat back, cup up
  pivot pin : on its flat
  hook L    : on its side
  hook R    : on its side

FITS
  eyes press into their sockets with 0.10 mm design clearance; a drop of CA if loose
  nose cap presses onto the muzzle peg; ears and antlers press into keyed sockets (0.20 mm per side)
  head slides over the plaque lip (0.5 mm gap) and is held by 3 x M3 x 16 flat head screws from the back into 2.5 mm pilots, 11 mm deep

HARDWARE
  1  SG90 or MG90S 9 g servo, its single arm horn and its two mounting screws (the horn screw is optional)
  1  ESP32-S3 SuperMini, stuck down with foam tape or hot glue
  1  NULLLAB NS4168 I2S amp (3W kit)
  1  the kit speaker: 30 mm square frame, ears 39 mm apart, 7.5 mm deep
  2  M3 x 6 screws for the amp (self tapping, or a machine screw that cuts its own thread), with M3 washers
  2  M2 x 6 self tapping screws for the speaker ears (or #2 x 1/4 in)
  3  M3 x 16 flat head (countersunk) screws, self tapping or machine, to hold the head to the plaque (or #4 x 5/8 in flat head wood screws)
  1  USB-C cable
     hookup wire and the kit's PH2.0 leads

ASSEMBLY
  1. press the eyes into their sockets, pointed corner toward the nose
  2. press the nose cap onto the peg at the end of the muzzle
  3. press the ears and antlers into their keyed sockets
  4. screw the servo to the fin with its own two screws, gear tower toward the right cheek; leave the horn off
  5. slide the speaker in under the amp shelf from the right side (the servo horn side): lift it over the seat ring, push it back against the stop and drop it; screw both ears down through the holes in the shelf
  6. slide the NS4168 in from the same side, components up, input connector leading; drop it onto the two spigots and screw it down with two M3 x 6 and washers, then plug in the speaker lead
  7. stick the controller onto the plinth on the left face of the fin, USB toward the plaque; the plinth is narrower than the pin rows, so every pin stays free. Wire it, and tuck the leads through the slot in the fin
  8. feed the USB-C cable through the plaque slot into the controller and lay it in the groove on the back
  9. slide the head over the fin onto the locating lip, then drive the three M3 x 16 flat head screws in from the back of the plaque until they sit flush. To take the head off later: slide the plaque off its hooks and undo those three screws
  10. drive the servo to its closed angle, reach in through the mouth and press the horn onto the spline pointing forward and down
  11. slide the jaw up and back through the mouth so its right arm takes the horn, then push the pivot pin in through the left cheek until it clicks
  12. roll the two printed hooks into the pegboard 4 inches apart, then slide the plaque onto them from the side

WIRING
  servo signal -> GPIO 7
  servo + -> 5V (USB)
  amp V -> 5V
  amp G, servo - -> GND
  amp BCLK -> GPIO 5
  amp LRCLK -> GPIO 6
  amp DIN -> GPIO 4
  speaker -> its PH2.0 plug into the amp; the output is bridge tied, never tie either leg to GND

fallback if a 3MF imports with one filament: open the STLs from the STL zip instead; every part is a single body.
